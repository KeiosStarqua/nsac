Ongoing HRP Research in Space
Human Investigations on NASA's Artemis II Mission
ARCHeR
A study on Artemis II monitored astronauts' well-being, activity, and sleep patterns to learn more about human health and performance in deep space.
Read About ARCHeR
Immune Biomarkers
Scientists analyzed blood and saliva samples from Artemis II crew members to see how deep space changes the immune system.
Read About Immune Biomarkers
Artemis II Standard Measures
Crews supplied a consistent set of health information to a data bank so that future researchers can learn more about astronaut health.
Read About Artemis II Standard Measures
Human Investigations in Low Earth Orbit
B-Complex
Investigating if a daily B vitamin supplement can prevent or mitigate brain and eye changes that may affect some humans in space
Read About B-Complex
Brain Fluid Pressure
Examining the relationship between fluid pressure inside the skull and changes to the eyes and brain of astronauts
Read About Brain Fluid Pressure
CIPHER
Providing a better understand of how the human body changes while orbiting the Earth via a suite of studies examining multiple body systems
Read About CIPHER
Simulating Manual Piloting for Moon Landings
Assessing how gravity transitions may affect the piloting capabilities of astronauts for future Moon landings
Read About Simulating Manual Piloting for Moon Landings
Spacecraft Occupant Protection
Surveying crew members' potential discomfort during re-entry to Earth and combining it with sensor data on spacecraft
Read About Spacecraft Occupant Protection
Spinal Fluid Biomarkers
Detecting substances in the spinal fluid of astronauts associated with space-related brain and eye changes
Read About Spinal Fluid Biomarkers
Standard Measures
Collecting a consistent set of physiological and psychological measurements to better understand how spaceflight affects the bodies and minds of crew members
Read About Standard Measures
Thigh Cuff
Testing if wearing tight thigh cuffs could keep more bodily fluids in the legs to help determine if future strategies can avert the eye disorders that can develop in space
Read About Thigh Cuff
Venous Haemostasis
Examining physical differences in astronauts that may contribute to blood flow and blood coagulation changes in space
Read About Venous Haemostasis
Zero T2
Examining how exercise in space without a treadmill affects human health and performance ahead of deep space missions
Read About Zero T2
Past Human Investigations
Altered Immunity
Analyzing blood, saliva, and urine samples to investigate how spaceflight alters the immune system
Read About Altered Immunity
Food Acceptability
Assessing how long-term spaceflight affects food appeal to improve current and future space food systems
Read About Food Acceptability
Food Physiology
Determining whether an enhanced spaceflight diet could help astronauts better adapt to space
Read About Food Physiology
Omics Archive
Collecting astronauts’ blood, urine, saliva, feces, and cells to document how long-duration spaceflight influences human health and performance and changes the body at the molecular level
Read About Omics Archive
Space Gardening
Surveying whether growing fresh vegetables in space can ease astronauts’ sense of isolation and confinement
Read About Space Gardening
Spinal Health
Scrutinizing scans of astronauts’ spines to inform strategies that counter bone and muscle loss in space
Read About Spinal Health
Tempus Pro
Test-driving a commercial telemedicine device used to collect and integrate health data, potentially enabling remote, near real-time diagnoses
Read About Tempus Pro
Veggie Monitoring
Monitoring microbial growth on hardware that stores crops in space to develop a better plant production system and ensure crew safety
Read About Veggie Monitoring
